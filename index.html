Abstract

Introduction

SLAMS “is a method used for autonomous vehicles that lets you build a map and localize your vehicle in that map at the same time.” (MathWorks (n.d.)) This technology/ algorithm is used in applications that require the vehicle to map out unknown environments.

SLAM’s plays major roles in critical systems/ situations where it is not feasible for humans to take part in; but how does it truly work? Autonomous vehicles are equipped with sensors (microphones, camera, temperature sensors). These sensors are used to detect changes in physical conditions, or environmental parameters and converts them into measurable signals. Sensors are essentially “eyes and ears” of autonomous vehicles and other machines (Abi Royen (2025)) (Muhammad Wicaksono and Shin, S.Y. (2025)). With this, the autonomous vehicle can use this data to understand the surroundings and then make decisions.

Analysis and discussion

With the definition of SLAMS established, we can discuss how these technologies are deployed across a wide range of systems. One use of this technology is in search and rescue, these types of applications perfectly demonstrate the usefulness of this technology, as in situation which emergency services would struggle to help people UAV’s can be used in place of humans (Muhammad Wicaksono and Shin, S.Y. (2025)). One major hurdle that arises in search and rescue operations is time constraints, as when there's an emergency e.g someone stuck or injured, every second matters. UAVs can be used in these operations to scout ahead, allowing the rescuers to plan more efficiently and optimise search coordination by clearing large areas of land to avoid wasting time (Piatt, L. (2023)).
